Three calls I'd make again

How I set direction, scale a product, and bring other teams along.

Strategy

AI-ready before anyone asked

Asked for a glossary. I built an AI foundation instead.

ResultIt now powers a support chatbot and a SQL generator.

Read the story
The situation
Leadership asked for a semantic layer. The easy version was a glossary: a definition for each dataset. That repeats a problem we already had. People defined terms, but nobody connected the data to make it useful.
My call
A glossary describes data. It doesn't solve semantics. AI needs rich, connected metadata to understand data, so I designed the platform as an AI foundation from day one, without waiting for a mandate.
What I did
Built connected metadata into the core: a catalog, lineage, and business vocabulary that links data to business domains instead of to the vendors it came from.
The result
That foundation now powers a support chatbot and a SQL generator that builds entities into our domain structure, moving us from vendor-based to domain-based data. It is ready to feed business knowledge graphs and other chatbots.
What I'd tell a team
Build for where the company is going, not only for the request in front of you.
Scale

Solving support before it hit

I predicted the bottleneck would be questions, not data.

ResultFewer tickets, and 3 to 10 hours a week back for the team.

Read the story
The situation
Ten engineers would serve 14 divisions. I knew the bottleneck wouldn't be the data. It would be questions: How do I use this? What data is there? How do I write this in SQL? How do I connect Tableau? Why am I getting this Python error?
My call
Answer the repeat questions before they reach a person, so engineers spend their time on work only they can do.
What I did
Built AI tools on the platform's own knowledge base, including a chatbot for support and user assistance.
The result
Fewer tickets, and users helping themselves, saving the team about 3 to 10 hours a week. The chat logs showed that the tickets still coming in are the complex ones that truly need a human.
What I'd tell a team
Read the logs. They show you where people actually need you.
Influence

Getting other teams to say yes

A data org pushed back. I made saying yes cheaper.

ResultThey came on board, and they've stayed engaged.

Read the story
The situation
The platform depends on upstream data teams getting their data ready. One data org pushed back: they felt we were asking a lot just to solve one team's problem.
My call
Don't escalate. Make it their win too, and make saying yes cheaper.
What I did
Showed them the impact of the work and how they would benefit. Then we offered our own resources: my team built libraries and helper tools that sped up the work we were asking them to do.
The result
They came on board, and they've stayed. I keep showing them how their data is used and the impact it has, so they feel part of the mission and take on new requests as they come.
What I'd tell a team
Influence is lowering the cost of yes, and sharing the credit after.
